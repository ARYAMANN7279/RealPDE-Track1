Correction to the notes

§11A's W and E are wrong

§11A records W = 0.6987 and E = 0.4919 for SOUP_v1, and that anchor gets quoted downstream. Recomputing W from scoring.py directly — S = 100/(1+0.5e), so 1−ñ(e) = 0.5S/(200−1.5S) — gives W = 0.68459, which matches the real anchor §19 measured (0.684538) to five decimals. The correct value is E = 0.5020. §19 already uses it; §11A contradicts it. Worth fixing before the next E projection is built on the wrong one.

What one subscore point is actually worth

Accuracy pays twice — directly, and again through W inside sps. But the W weights are 0.5·dm + 0.3·tke + 0.2·mvpe, so rel_l2 carries the largest share of W, not tke.

In raw-error space this is a 3.42:1 rel_l2-to-tke ratio, against the 3.04:1 that set w ∈ [0.05, 0.15] in §8. That sits inside §8's own plateau, so it is a nudge, not a lever — worth stating plainly rather than overselling. The real finding in this table is mvpe at 0.277, second only to rel_l2, and never optimised.

The plan

Four levers with no measurement against them

Every route in §19A is a bounds signal fitted post-hoc to a frozen model, and that family is genuinely closed. None of the following is in that family, and none appears in §7's ruled-out table or anywhere else in the notes.

01

Put mvpe in the fine-tuning loss

never tested≈ +0.18 to +0.35

Grepping project_memory.md and RESEARCH_aug18 for mvpe as a training target returns nothing — no loss term, no experiment, no ruled-out row. It has moved +0.03 across every fine-tune ever run, i.e. it has been along for the ride. At 0.277 per point it is the second most valuable subscore we have, and we sit at 92.87 against a cohort median of 93.53 and a board max of 94.13.

It is also unusually cheap to attack. mvpe_rel_l2_per_sample is a time-averaged relative error over at most 4 probe x-stations × 9 probe y-rows in the near wake — roughly 36 fixed pixels out of 8,192. A loss term on that region barely perturbs rel_l2, and because it is time-averaged it rewards getting the mean field right rather than the instantaneous one.

§7's own prescription for the spectral correction was "do it as a fine-tuning loss term instead", after the post-hoc version lost 1.47 time points. Same argument applies here.

02

Shrink the bounds head 46×

measured, never shipped≈ +0.10

§20 measured that a 13k-parameter head beats the shipped 606k one on both splits (+0.0552 vs +0.0487 under Re-extrapolation) and then filed it as "not slot-worthy" because the edge transfers at 10%. That reasoning missed the latency side: ROBUST scored time 91.36 with constant bounds; SOUP_v1 scored 90.32 with the head. A model soup is a weight average — identical architecture, identical FLOPs to its members — so that 1.04-point time loss is the head's inference path, not the soup's. Shrinking it recovers most of it, and improves the head on the measurement we already have. It rides along with any other submission at no extra cost.

03

All 81 trajectories and a longer schedule

gap in the §14 searchunquantified

§14 declared the recipe exhausted on seven axes, but the two relevant experiments were run separately. Experiment A varied the data (72 vs 64 trajectories) at fixed steps — and its own note says the result "under-trains at fixed steps", which is a confound, not a finding. Experiment B varied the schedule on the 64-trajectory split. More data with a schedule scaled to match it was never run.

Board corroboration: exp172_t1_pointtcn_all81_… appears twice inside the top 25 (simon-zhou #7, fluidsplat #23). "all81" is in the filename.

04

EMA and dropout in the fine-tune

board-corroboratedunquantified

The #1 submission on the board is called opt_ft12dp02_ema999_tkehead_b210.zip. Read it: dp02 = dropout 0.2, ema999 = EMA 0.999, plus a dedicated tke head. Neither dropout nor EMA appears anywhere in our notes. §8's model soup averages endpoints across separate runs; EMA averages along a single trajectory — a different estimator with different variance behaviour under distribution shift, and the one axis of "weight averaging" §14 did not vary. Both are one-line changes to finetune.py.

05

Condition on angle of attack

our own next hypothesisunquantified

§20 lists three untested explanations for the unexplained 90% transfer loss and puts angle-of-attack shift first — only Reynolds was ever tested, and Reynolds was refuted. Independently, spochinapeddi sits at 80.00 with sub14_peraoa_lead.zip — per-AoA — on a tke of 75.69, which is below ours, and reaches E = 0.5788. That is a team beating us by 1.5 with a worse model and an AoA-aware method, corroborating the hypothesis our own notes queued next.

06

Bracket the LUT width from below

logical gap± 0.05

§19 concluded width 1.00 is proven optimal from two real anchors: 1.00 and 1.25. Both sit on the same side of the peak. That bounds the optimum at ≤ 1.25; it does not locate it at 1.00 — the rest of the argument leans on the local curve's peak position transferring, which is the exact assumption §20 warns has failed four times. One anchor near 0.85 closes it properly. Low value, but it is a real anchor and slots are not scarce.
